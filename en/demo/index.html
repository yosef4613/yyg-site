How it works, on an example project

I took a fictional carpentry shop called "Anaf" and put it through exactly what every client goes through: a discovery questionnaire, a quote, 3 completely different designs for the same content, and a feedback form that picks a direction. The business is fictional. The questionnaires, the quote and the designs are real, and you can open each of them.

The discovery questionnaire

The client filled in the questionnaire on the site in about 10 minutes, and attached the logo and 4 photos of their work. These are the answers that shaped the design most:

The business

A family carpentry shop building custom solid-wood furniture: kitchens, wardrobes, bookcases and tables, for homes and synagogues.

What sets them apart

We measure at the client's home and show a visualization before we start. We build everything ourselves from solid wood, and whoever builds it also installs it. A 10-year warranty.

The customers

Private customers, institutions and organizations, a religious community. Mostly a religious and Haredi audience: photos without people, clean language, and an emphasis on solutions for Shabbat and holidays, such as extending tables and room for dishes.

The goal of the site

Order or request a quote. 5 to 10 requests a month to book a measurement, and no more explaining the same things over and over on the phone.

3 words the site should convey

Natural, warm and personal, professional and trustworthy. Background: I'd like to see both.

What they don't want to see

Cluttered sites, pop-ups, and stock photos of smiling people.

Pages

Home, about, services, work and gallery, testimonials, FAQ, contact
