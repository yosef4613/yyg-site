A synagogue website: prayer times, a renovation campaign and dedications

A fictional synagogue called "Nachalat Yitzchak" in Beit Shemesh, a community of about 120 families. The gabbai asked for two things: to update the prayer times himself from his phone, and a campaign to renovate the sanctuary, with card donations and dedications. We went through the whole process: a questionnaire, a quote, 3 designs, feedback, the final version and the admin screen.

A fictional example synagogue: the name, details, donors and amounts are made up, and no donation is charged. The images are 3D renders I built specially, and the emblem was redrawn for the example.

The discovery questionnaire

The gabbai filled in the questionnaire in about 10 minutes. He had only a photo of the old emblem from the sign at the entrance, and no photos at all. These are the answers that shaped the work most:

The community

A neighborhood synagogue in Beit Shemesh, with a community of about 120 families. Two morning minyanim, afternoon and evening prayers every day, Torah classes all through the week, and a small hall for family celebrations.

What sets them apart

A warm community where everyone knows everyone. A minyan that starts on time. A rabbi who is there for every family, and classes for every level, from daf yomi to father and son learning on Saturday nights.

Who it's for

A religious community, parents, older people, english speakers. Worshippers need to know when the minyan and the class are. Families who celebrated with us, and relatives in Israel and abroad, want an easy way to donate and to dedicate something in memory of their parents, and to get a receipt.

What people ask the gabbai

When the second morning minyan is, when Mincha is this week, how to book the hall for a celebration, how much it costs to dedicate a bench or a plaque on the memorial board, and whether there's a receipt for tax purposes.

What the gabbai wants to update himself

The prayer times every week, especially Mincha and Maariv, which move with sunset. The classes, community announcements and campaign updates. I want to do it myself from my phone, on Saturday night.

Sensitivities

No pictures of people at all. The photos we have show worshippers, and we don't publish those. No holy names in the text, and no verses on the images. Donors appear by family name, and anyone who wants to can stay anonymous.

How they'll know it's working

That we reach the ₪350,000 for renovating the sanctuary, and that more families give a small monthly donation. And that people stop asking me in shul when Mincha is.

Package, budget and timing

Package: Business website (from ₪4,900)

The final version

The gabbai chose design 3 and paid 40%. I built the site on design 3 and added exactly what he asked for from the other designs: the wooden prayer-times board from design 1, and the "this week at shul" strip and the fixed donate button on phones from design 2. Both were adapted to design 3's stone colors and fonts. I also added what he said was missing: candle lighting and the end of Shabbat, which he types in himself every week. From here the gabbai approves, pays the final 30%, and the site goes live. From that day on he updates the times, the classes and the campaign himself.

Example

What changed after the feedback

  • The wooden prayer-times board from design 1, like the board at the entrance: in stone and copper colors, with the weekly note pinned underneath
  • The "this week at shul" strip from design 2, under the opening: the next prayer, today's classes and the campaign's stone wall
  • On phones, a "donate" button fixed at the bottom, showing how much has been raised. It hides near the donation form and while typing
  • Candle lighting and the end of Shabbat on the Shabbat board. The gabbai types them in every week on the update screen, and an empty field simply doesn't show
  • Polish in the form: picking an item to dedicate selects its price, so no old amount stays highlighted

The gabbai's screen

This is why the site has a gabbai's screen: the times move with sunset, the classes change, and the campaign is alive every day. On this screen the gabbai updates everything himself, from his phone, in five minutes on Saturday night. He presses "save", and the site updates right away.

  • Weekday and Shabbat prayer times, including candle lighting, the end of Shabbat and the weekly note
  • A sunset shortcut: Mincha and Maariv 5 minutes earlier or later, in one tap
  • Classes and community announcements: add, edit and delete
  • The campaign: the goal, the end date, updates for the community, and donations that came in as cash or a cheque
  • Dedications: prices, what's already dedicated, and new names on the memorial board
