A nonprofit website, with card donations and a campaign that updates itself

A fictional nonprofit called "Sal Bracha", which brings Shabbat and holiday food baskets to about 400 families in Jerusalem. Until now it only took cash and bank transfers. It asked for a site where people can give by card, one-time or monthly, with a winter campaign, a goal meter and a donor list that update themselves. We went through the whole process: a questionnaire, a quote, 3 designs, feedback, the final version and the admin screen.

A fictional example nonprofit: the name, details, donors and testimonials are made up, and no donation is charged. The images are 3D renders I built specially.

The discovery questionnaire

The nonprofit's chairman filled in the questionnaire in about 10 minutes. He had only a photo of the logo, and no photos at all, since the families are never photographed. These are the answers that shaped the work most:

The organization

A small Jerusalem nonprofit that brings Shabbat and holiday food baskets to about 400 families. Volunteers pack every Thursday evening, and the baskets reach the door on Friday morning, quietly and with dignity.

What sets them apart

Almost every shekel goes to food: we have no office, and the people who pack and deliver are volunteers. Baskets reach the door with no sign and no photos, so no family has to feel ashamed. And donors know exactly what they gave: ₪180 is one Shabbat basket for one family.

The donors

Private customers, business owners, a religious community. Donors want to give easily, and to know the money arrived. Many want to give by monthly standing order, and to dedicate the donation in memory of someone or for a recovery. And volunteers need to know when and where we pack.

Sensitivities

The families' dignity comes first: no photos, names or stories that identify a family. No photos of people at all. Clean, gentle language, without stirring pity and without words like "the poor".

The goal of the site

Donate by card, one-time or monthly. That at least a third of the winter campaign donations come through the site, and that we reach 100 monthly donors by the end of the year. And that I stop sending bank details on WhatsApp, one by one.

What he wants to update himself

The campaign: title, goal and dates. A short update from the field after every Thursday. Cash and check donations that come in by hand, so the goal meter stays accurate. And seeing who donated.

3 words the site should convey

Warm and personal, professional and trustworthy, calm and clean. Background: Light.

The final version

The client chose design 2 and paid 40%. I built the site on design 2, with three things from the other designs: the engraved donor board from design 1, where every amount is the same size, the basket picture from design 1 in the opening, next to the meter, and the Thursday evening invitation from design 3. All of them were adapted to the navy, saffron and typeface of design 2. I also added what he asked for in the feedback form: a clear statement that the families' names are never revealed, and that there are no photos of them. From here the client approves, pays the final 50%, and the site goes live. From that day on he runs the campaign himself.

Example

What changed after the feedback

  • The engraved donor board from design 1, in navy and saffron: names centered, and every amount in the same small size
  • The basket picture from design 1 in the opening, with the big meter over it: you see what a donation buys
  • The Thursday evening invitation from design 3, as a paper ticket on the navy background
  • A clear statement in the "Where the money goes" section: the families' names are never revealed, and there are no photos of them
  • A new FAQ question: "Are there names or photos of the families on the site?"

The campaign admin screen

This is why we chose a website with an admin area: card donations come in by themselves, but much of the giving still arrives as cash and cheques, and the chairman wants to write an update after every Thursday. On this screen he adds a donation that came in by hand, writes a field update, changes the goal and the dates, and sees who gave. The goal meter, the countdown and the donor list on the site update right away.

  • A donation that came in by hand: cash, cheque or transfer, named or anonymous, with a dedication
  • A field update after every Thursday, with ready-made openings
  • The donor list: hide a name or delete a donation, always with a confirmation first
  • The campaign: title, goal and dates, plus a snapshot of today, this week and the monthly donors
  • A CSV file for the treasurer, for receipts and bookkeeping
