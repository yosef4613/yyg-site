Websites for catering and food businesses

A website that shows your food at its best, answers the repeat questions in advance, and turns a visitor into an organized order on WhatsApp or by email.

Who it's for

  • Event catering, dessert bars, bakeries and designed cakes.
  • Platters, gift boxes and food delivery.
  • A bakery, an eatery or a private chef who takes orders in advance.

What a food business website needs

  • Real photos of your food. They sell better than any text.
  • An organized menu or catalog by category, with a page for each main product.
  • Answers to the repeat questions: kosher certification, minimum order, how far ahead to order, delivery areas, and a starting price or range.
  • Easy ordering: a WhatsApp button with a ready message, and next to it a form that sends the request by email, for people without WhatsApp.
  • Speed on a phone: a site with many photos has to be built right, or it loads slowly and loses people.

An example from my work: HaKef SheBaAfiya

For the boutique dessert caterer HaKef SheBaAfiya, I built a website with a catalog by category and a page for each product, plus a bar planner: the customer picks the number of guests, the type of event, kosher level and serving style, and gets an estimate of quantities by type of dessert. From the planner, they continue to an organized request with all the details and the calculation, sent on WhatsApp or straight to the business's email. There's also a recipe template the owner updates on her own. The new site loads three times faster on a phone than the old one.

What to prepare

  • Photos of your food, as real as possible. Professional photography isn't included, but good phone photos in daylight can work nicely too.
  • A list of products or dishes, with a price or price range if you publish prices.
  • Your kosher certification details, if any.
  • Delivery areas, minimum order, and how far ahead people need to order.

Common mistakes on food business websites

  • Stock photos instead of your real food. Customers notice quickly, and it hurts trust.
  • A menu as a PDF file that's hard to read and zoom on a phone. A menu built into the site is easier, and Google can read it too.
  • No price at all: without even a starting price or range, some people simply won't reach out.
  • Phone-only ordering during limited hours. Someone planning an event in the evening needs a way to leave a request right away.
