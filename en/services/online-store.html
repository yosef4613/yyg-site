Who an online store is for

  • A business that sells products at fixed prices and wants to be paid up front.
  • Anyone who sells today over WhatsApp or the phone, and answers the same questions again and again: what's available, how much, and how to pay.
  • Anyone who wants to ship nationwide, or offer organized self-pickup.

If every order is custom and has no fixed price, like a designed cake or an event, a site with a catalog and a quote request is sometimes better. That's how the HaKef SheBaAfiya site works. More in websites for catering and food businesses.

What to prepare for a store

  • A product list: name, price, a short description and a photo. A simple spreadsheet is enough.
  • An account with a payment provider. It's worth opening one right at the start, because approval takes a few days.
  • A shipping and returns policy: shipping cost, delivery time, and how returns work. I prepare the terms based on that, and you approve them.

Common mistakes in small stores

  • A shipping cost that only shows up at the end. It's better to show it on the product page.
  • Inconsistent photos: different backgrounds, lighting and sizes make the store look messy.
  • A long checkout: every unnecessary step loses buyers. Checkout should be short and easy on a phone.
  • No clear returns policy to reassure first-time buyers.
