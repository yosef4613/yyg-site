Landing page or full website?

What sets them apart, who each one is for, and how to decide without paying for what you don’t need.

Yehuda Gordon, web designer · Updated November 2026

This is one of the first questions business owners ask. The answer comes down to a simple question: how many different things do you want customers to do, and how much do you have to say?

What is a landing page?

One long, focused page that leads to a single action: calling, leaving details, or signing up. There’s no menu with lots of pages, and nothing to distract.

A landing page is the right fit when:

  • You have one main service or a specific promotion.
  • You want to get started fast (with me, up to 4 working days) and on a small budget (from ₪2,000).
  • You’re running ads or handing out flyers, and you want everyone who arrives to do one thing.

What is a full website?

Several pages with a menu: home, services, about, FAQ, contact, and sometimes a content section too. With an admin panel, you update anything that changes on your own.

A full website is the right fit when:

  • You have several services, and each one needs its own explanation.
  • You want to show up on Google for several different searches. Each page gets its own chance to appear.
  • You have content that changes: news, schedules, promotions, or projects.

What if you need sign-ups or sales?

Organizations, institutions, and class providers that take sign-ups need a website with sign-ups & mailing list: a sign-up form, user accounts, and automatic emails. And if you sell products, you need an online store with a cart, credit card payments, and shipping.

Frequently asked questions

Can I start with a landing page and expand later?

Yes. The landing page becomes the home page, or one of the pages, of the full website, so you don’t start from scratch.

Will a landing page show up on Google?

Yes, but usually for fewer searches than a full website, because it has less content and fewer pages.
