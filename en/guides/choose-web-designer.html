How to choose who builds your website

10 questions worth asking before you pay, and what a good answer looks like. Even if you don’t end up choosing me.

Yehuda Gordon, web designer · Updated November 2026

Why ask before you pay

Most website disappointments don't start with the design. They start with a different understanding of what exactly you're buying: who writes the copy, how many revisions are included, and what it costs after the first year. A fifteen-minute call with these questions saves months of misunderstandings. You can ask them of any web designer, even if you don't end up choosing me.

The 10 questions

1. What exactly is included in the price?

How many pages, who writes the copy, whether there's a form, mobile optimization, Google readiness and an accessibility statement. A good answer is a written list in the quote, not "everything is included".

2. Does the price include VAT?

"From ₪3,000" can become ₪3,540 once VAT is added. Ask for the final price, as one number. My prices are final, and no VAT is added.

3. Will I see a design before I pay most of the price?

In the market, people often pay 30% to 50% before they see anything. Ask how many designs you'll see, and what you pay at each stage. With me, you start with 10%, see 3 complete designs with your own content, and only after you choose do you pay another 40%.

4. How many revision rounds are included, and what counts as a revision?

Changing a color, a photo or some text is a revision. Changing the whole structure after you've approved it is new work. A good answer defines the number of rounds, and what goes into them.

5. Whose name will the domain and website be in?

The domain should be registered in your name, and the website should be yours, so you can move to another company without losing it. This is one of the most important questions, because it's hard to fix later.

6. What does it cost after the first year?

Domain, hosting, business email, plugin licenses and maintenance. Ask for one yearly number. With me, the first year is included, and after that it's ₪590 a year with All-in Care, or with your own hosting company.

7. Can I update it myself, and who teaches me?

Ask exactly what you'll be able to change yourself: prices, photos, promotions. And ask whether there's training and a written guide, or whether every change will cost you money.

8. How long will it take, and from which day do you count?

"Two weeks" can mean two weeks from the day you pay, or from the day the designer has all the materials. It's worth knowing up front. More on this in the guide how long it takes to build a website.

9. What happens after launch?

How long support and bug fixes last, who backs up the site, and who you contact if something stops working. With me, there are 4 months of support after launch.

10. Can I see live websites you've built?

Not just screenshots: the address of a working site that you can open on your phone and check how fast it loads. And if possible, talk to a client.

Signs to stop and check

  • A price without a list of what's included.
  • A domain registered in the designer's name.
  • "We'll do the copy later", without saying who will write it.
  • Pressure to sign today, "because the offer ends".
  • No live website to show.

The cheapest quote isn't always the cheapest in the end, if you pay for everything separately in the second year. And the most expensive isn't always the best, if it has no clear list of what you get.

What to ask yourself before you reach out

  • What should the website achieve? Calls, sign-ups, orders, or getting to know you.
  • Who are your customers? Age, area, and which device they use, usually a phone.
  • Roughly how many pages? One service or many, and whether there's content that changes.
  • Who will update the site? You, someone on your team, or the designer.
  • What's the budget and the deadline? Even a rough range helps you get an accurate quote.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important thing to check before choosing a web designer?

That the domain and website will be in your name, and that there's a written list of what the price includes. Everything else can be fixed later; that's hard to fix.

Is it OK to pay a deposit?

Yes, a deposit is normal. It's worth knowing up front what it pays for and when you'll see a design. With me, you start with 10%, and for that you get 3 complete designs.

How can I tell if a website someone built is fast?

Open it on your phone, ideally not on fast Wi-Fi, and see how long it takes until the content appears. You can also check it with Google's free PageSpeed Insights tool.

Is a low price a bad sign?

Not necessarily. But check what's included, who writes the copy, and what the second year costs. Sometimes that's where the difference is.

How many quotes should I ask for?

Two or three are enough to understand the market. More than that usually just confuses.
